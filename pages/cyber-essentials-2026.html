Cyber Security

Cyber Essentials in 2026: what the latest updates mean for you

4 June 20265 min read

Cyber Essentials remains one of the simplest, most effective ways for UK organisations to demonstrate they take security seriously. But the scheme keeps moving — and the 2026 update brings changes worth understanding.

For many organisations, certification is no longer optional. It is increasingly written into contracts, tender requirements and insurance policies. The good news is that the fundamentals haven't changed: it is still about getting the basics right, consistently.

What's changed this year

The latest revision sharpens the focus on how modern organisations actually work — distributed teams, cloud-first infrastructure and the blurred line between corporate and personal devices.

  • Clearer expectations around cloud service configuration and shared-responsibility models.
  • Tighter guidance on multi-factor authentication for administrative and cloud accounts.
  • Updated treatment of home-working devices within the certification boundary.

Why it matters beyond the badge

It is tempting to treat certification as a box-ticking exercise. In practice, the controls map directly to the threats that cause the most disruption: phishing, unpatched systems and weak access management.

Certification should be the floor, not the ceiling — a baseline you build on, not a one-off project.

The organisations that get the most value treat the annual assessment as a prompt to review their wider posture, not just to renew a certificate.

Getting there without the headache

We guide organisations through the whole process — from gap analysis to remediation and submission — and keep you compliant year-round, not just at renewal. The aim is simple: confidence that the basics are handled, so your team can focus on the work that matters.
